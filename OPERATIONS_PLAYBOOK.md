# Operations Playbook

## Operating law

The route is the product. Great branding cannot rescue scattered stops, ambiguous instructions, or missing proof.

## Route-cell statuses

- Research.
- Waitlist.
- Opening.
- Active.
- Capacity full.
- Premium review.
- Closed.

## Acceptance

Accept when the address is serviceable, schedule aligns, access is safe, route capacity exists, and the customer accepts the window and terms. Waitlist or quote when density, distance, complexity, or capacity is wrong. Decline unsafe access, entering occupied living space, unsecured aggressive animals, exact-time demands, or economics that do not work.

## Route economics

```text
estimated_route_time = drive_time + sum(expected_task_time) + exception_buffer
route_contribution = route_revenue - direct_labor - mileage_cost - payment_and_message_cost - variable_support
revenue_per_route_hour = route_revenue / actual_route_hours
contribution_per_task = route_contribution / completed_tasks
```

Do not use a fixed "five neighbors" threshold as the economic model. Store expected task time by property complexity and replace assumptions with actuals every two weeks during pilot.

## Planning times

Initial assumptions for measurement, not promises:

- Standard accessible stop: 3-5 minutes.
- Side yard/gate: 5-8 minutes.
- Long/steep driveway: 8-12 minutes.
- Garage or complicated access: 8-15 minutes.

Rollout and return are separate operational commitments.

## Weekly planning

- Verify collection/holiday schedules.
- Apply pauses and property changes.
- Generate cycles and tasks.
- Review unverified schedule data.
- Check capacity, weather, and worker availability.
- Lock/publish routes as late as necessary for accuracy and early enough for worker planning.

## Rollout SOP

1. Review task and hazard notes.
2. Confirm correct property and bins.
3. Move approved bins to approved placement.
4. Follow hauler spacing/orientation rules.
5. Capture proof photo.
6. Complete or record exception.

## Return SOP

1. Confirm collection occurred.
2. If not, record hauler delay/partial collection and follow recheck rule.
3. Return bins to approved storage point.
4. Capture proof photo.
5. Complete or record exception.

## Gate/access failure

- Record failed method without exposing the secret.
- Send secure update request to authorized contact.
- A configurable response window may move the task into a high-priority retry if operationally feasible.
- Never guarantee the runner will return immediately.
- Close with documented result and customer message.

## Weather and safety

Runners may stop work for ice, lightning, wildfire restrictions, aggressive animals, unsafe traffic, unstable terrain, excessive weight, or any credible hazard. Admin communicates delay/reschedule. No route metric penalizes a good-faith safety stop.

## Hauler delay

Do not waste repeated visits without evidence. Use route/area signals, customer/runner reports, and a configurable recheck policy. Customer sees "collection delayed" rather than a fake completed return.

## CurbSitter onDemand SOP

Accept only in active route cells with capacity. Verify up to 3 bins, provider/day, rollout and return locations, access, and contact details. Generate the same two-task cycle, proof, exception, and notification rules used for subscriptions.

## Minor javelina/wind reset

When discovered during a scheduled visit, upright the bin, recover a minor immediate spill that can be handled safely within the stop, photograph the corrected condition, and continue. Escalate or quote widespread debris, sharp/hazardous material, excessive cleanup, or any separate dispatch.

## Core KPIs

- Cycle completion/proof rate.
- Preventable missed-service rate.
- Exceptions by category and property.
- Revenue/contribution per route hour and mile.
- Task time by complexity.
- On-time return within published window.
- Photo upload failure/retry.
- Support contacts per 100 cycles.
- Churn, referral share, review rate, and route-cell density.
