| D-024 | Onboarding shows customers a confirmed pickup date, not just a collection weekday, for both subscriptions and CurbSitter onDemand once the property's collection day is known — reversing the WIP "no service date at signup, day of week only" approach from the in-progress onboarding redesign. Any customer-facing date is always labeled "pickup date"; rollout (the evening before) is shown as a separate operational confirmation line, never presented as a competing "actual" date. **First-visit lead-time floor, confirmed 2026-08-05: 2 days** (`SERVICE_WINDOWS.firstPickupLeadDays`). Rollout happens the evening before collection, so without a floor a signup whose collection day is tomorrow would require same-evening rollout at a property no runner has visited. Because pickup recurs weekly, the floor pushes anyone whose day falls inside it to the following week — 2 is the smallest value that avoids same-evening rollout, so it delays the fewest customers. The floor governs **both** the date shown at signup and the date `generateTasksForOrder` actually books, from one shared computation, so the two can never diverge. | Locked (2026-08-04; floor confirmed 2026-08-05) | Owner reversal: customers expect to see a scheduled date, not just a weekday. Reuses the "pickup date" + rollout-evening-confirmation wording already built and validated for onDemand under PP-14, which exists specifically because an earlier "what date do you need service?" draft confused customers about rollout vs. pickup. The floor is an operational-honesty guard: under D-027 a clean onDemand signup schedules at payment, so a display-only floor would show one date and book another. | Owner ADR to reverse. |
| D-025 | Collection-day verification is automated by cross-checking the customer's onboarding answer against the City of Prescott's public "Solid Waste Route Days" ArcGIS Feature Service (`Current_Day` layer, synced/cached locally rather than queried live per signup — 16 polygons total). A match auto-verifies (`collection_schedules.verification_status = 'verified'`), skipping admin review of that field entirely. A mismatch shows the customer a conflict prompt (city's day vs. their stated day); if they reaffirm their own answer, signup proceeds and both values are surfaced to admin as `needs_review`. An address the City lookup can't find at all gets routed to contact support rather than silently proceeding. | Locked (2026-08-04, revised 2026-08-04 — see the D-025 revision row below) | The City publishes this as an unauthenticated, freely queryable ArcGIS Feature Service; confirmed live against the public lookup tool during evaluation (`services5.arcgis.com/.../Solid_Waste_Route_Days/FeatureServer/3`). No explicit reuse license or terms are published by the City for this service. | Owner ADR to reverse; confirm reuse terms with City of Prescott Solid Waste before production reliance. |
| D-025a | **D-025 revision.** The City lookup runs once at stage 1, as soon as the address is known, instead of being triggered by the stage-3 day question. An "I'm not sure" collection-day answer is resolved from that lookup when the City covers the address, and that day becomes the property's collection day. When the City has no record and the customer doesn't know their day, signup still proceeds and an admin resolves the day by hand (via the hauler's own public schedule tool) before approval — the customer is never turned away. A customer who states a **private hauler** is trusted unconditionally, regardless of what City records show. **Automated querying of private hauler APIs is rejected** — WM's pickup-schedule lookup (`rest-api.wm.com/account/search` → `/services` → `/pickupinfo`) is access-controlled (HTTP 401 without an Okta token), keyed by Google Place ID, and returns third-party customer *account* records rather than open geographic data; using it programmatically would require replaying a lifted credential against WM's Terms of Use. Manual admin lookup in the hauler's own public tool is the sanctioned path. | Locked (2026-08-04) | An unsure customer with City coverage can be answered instantly instead of queued, and one without coverage shouldn't be turned away when a 30-second manual lookup resolves it. The WM rejection is a legal/ToS boundary, not a technical one: the City publishes route-day *zones* as open government GIS data, while WM's endpoint exposes customer relationships behind an access control. | Owner ADR to reverse. Automating any hauler API requires documented, granted access from that hauler first. |
| D-027 | **Narrows D-018.** A signup activates immediately at payment, with no admin click, when both facts a desk can actually verify check out: the collection day is settled (a City-verified match, a day the City supplied for a customer who didn't know it, or a self-reported day with no City record to contradict it) **and** parcel records confirm the property is residential. Any other case — a customer-reaffirmed day conflict, a day nobody has established yet, or a property flagged or unverifiable as residential — still requires admin approval before activation, unchanged from D-018. Hazard presence does not gate this either way. Automatically activated signups receive a `service_confirmed` notification (not `review_approved`, which implies a human acted) and are recorded in the audit log with a null actor. | Locked (2026-08-04) | A desk review cannot verify anything hazard- or access-related without a site visit — D-026 already established that. The one fact that *is* verifiable pre-visit, residential vs. commercial status, is now automated from the same class of government GIS data D-025 uses. Requiring a human click where nothing is actually being verified adds customer-facing uncertainty (paid, then silence) without a matching safety benefit. | Owner ADR to reverse. |
| D-026 | Admin no longer reviews hazard/access-instruction flags (gate, garage, steep grade, animals, poor lighting, ice, etc.) before first service — this removes the prior `requiresAccessReview` gate on gate/garage hazards specifically. `accessSecretNotes` must meet a minimum length whenever `hazards` includes `gate` or `garage`, enforced by onboarding schema validation instead of manual review. Any access or hazard problem discovered at the property is handled by the runner and existing exception tooling, the same as any other on-site surprise. | Locked (2026-08-04) | Admin review could never verify a gate code's correctness before a runner is physically on-site — the review step added delay without adding real safety margin. The one legitimate gap it caught (blank or garbage access notes) is better enforced by validation than by a human queue. Combined with D-025, admin review is now scoped to the collection-day-mismatch case only. | Owner ADR to reintroduce manual hazard review. |
